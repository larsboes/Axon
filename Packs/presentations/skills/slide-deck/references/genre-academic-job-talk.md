# Genre profile: academic job talk

Use this profile for a faculty or postdoc job talk. A search committee uses that talk to
decide. Do not use it for a defence (`genre-dsr-defence.md`) or a paper talk
(`genre-empirical-cs-talk.md`). Its question differs from both.

Read `academic-narrative.md` first. This file covers the talk shape.

## The room

The committee decides one thing: whether they want to work with this person for the next
six years. The talk is therefore about a research programme. It is not about a paper or a
thesis.

- The audience is mixed. One or two specialists work in your area, and several do not.
  The non-specialists vote.
- The talk usually runs 45 to 60 minutes, with questions during and after.
- The committee reads the past work anyway. The talk's job is the future, grounded in the
  past.
- Teaching usually has its own talk or its own section. Leave it out of the research talk
  unless the format asks for it.

## The spine

```
the problem I work on         one sentence a non-specialist can follow
why it matters                the stake
what I have shown             the past work, compressed into evidence
the through-line              the one idea that connects the projects
the next five years           a concrete agenda
how I work                    methods, collaborators, students
why here                      the fit, stated once and specifically
```

This is the one academic genre whose spine runs programme, evidence, agenda. A job talk
built on the paper spine reads as a dissertation recap. That is the standard failure.

## What must land

1. **A research identity in one sentence.** The committee repeats it to each other after
   the talk. When they cannot, nothing else counted.
2. **The through-line.** Name it. Do not make the committee infer the connection between
   three projects.
3. **A concrete agenda.** Two or three directions, each with a first result, a
   collaborator, or a dataset already in hand. "I will continue to explore X" is not an
   agenda.
4. **The fit.** One slide, specific about the people, the group, the infrastructure and
   the teaching need. Flattery is not fit.
5. **Feasibility.** Separate what you can do alone in year one from what needs the group.

## Q&A

Answer shape: claim, evidence, boundary. The job-talk questions concern the programme
rather than the method.

| the question | what it asks |
|---|---|
| "What will you work on first?" | whether the agenda is concrete |
| "How is this different from your advisor's work?" | whether you have your own programme |
| "Where does the money come from?" | whether this is fundable |
| "How would you involve students?" | whether you will be a colleague |
| "Why not just extend the thesis?" | a test you should pass without hesitating |
| "What if the direction fails?" | your judgement, and your second line of work |

## Traps

- Do not recap the dissertation. The past work is evidence for the programme, so it gets a
  fraction of the time a paper talk gives it.
- Do not overclaim the agenda. A plan with no risk reads as naive. Name the risky step and
  the fallback.
- Do not hide the through-line and hope the committee finds it.
- Do not spend the talk on teaching unless the format combines the two.
- Do not close on a thank-you. Close on the research identity, in the same sentence as the
  opening, one level more concrete.
